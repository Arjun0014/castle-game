# LORE.md

# Working Canon

The names in this document are working canon for implementation and environmental storytelling. They may be renamed later, but the underlying relationships should remain stable unless intentionally redesigned.

## Core names

- **Kingdom:** Veyr
- **Castle:** Caer Veyr
- **Royal dynasty:** House Vaelor
- **Ancient temporal mechanism:** the Crownheart
- **The catastrophe:** the Sundering
- **The protagonist:** the Uncrowned
- **The final king:** King Aldren Vaelor
- **The king's preserved boss-form:** the Last Crown

---

# 1. The Kingdom of Veyr

For centuries, Veyr was ruled from Caer Veyr, an enormous fortress-palace built around an object far older than the kingdom itself.

The founders of House Vaelor discovered the object beneath the oldest foundations of the castle.

They called it the **Crownheart**.

It was not originally a weapon.

It possessed a stranger property: it could retain a perfect spatial memory of places surrounding it.

Stone, doors, halls, objects, and eventually people could leave an imprint in that memory.

The first Vaelor rulers learned to influence it.

They performed a royal binding in which the ruling bloodline was linked to the Crownheart. The ritual allowed certain castle mechanisms to recognize royal blood and gave the rulers limited influence over the Crownheart's preserved memories.

Over generations, the court turned this into mythology.

The public story was simple:

> The castle remembers its rightful king.

---

# 2. The Price of the Binding

The royal family believed they controlled the Crownheart.

In reality, each generation made the bond stronger.

The castle remembered more.

The bloodline became more dependent on it.

Royal rites, succession ceremonies, sealed doors, defensive systems, and hidden chambers were gradually built around the connection.

By the final generations of House Vaelor, the royal line and the castle were no longer truly separate systems.

The Crownheart recognized the blood.

The blood carried the Crownheart's resonance.

This is why the protagonist can affect the castle centuries later.

---

# 3. The Last Days

During the reign of **King Aldren Vaelor**, Veyr entered a prolonged war.

An enemy force eventually reached Caer Veyr.

History says the kingdom fell during the siege.

That is only partly true.

Aldren had discovered something in the royal archives:

The Crownheart might be capable of preserving not merely a room, but the entire castle.

If enough living resonance were bound to it, Caer Veyr could theoretically be held forever at a chosen moment.

The king became convinced that allowing the kingdom to disappear would be a greater crime than sacrificing the people currently inside it.

He ordered the deepest chambers sealed.

He moved soldiers into sections of the castle they did not understand.

He ordered excavations around the Crownheart.

Servants disappeared.

Royal guards began preventing people from leaving certain wings.

Some members of the court discovered the plan and attempted to stop him.

By the time the enemy army reached the walls, the castle was already tearing itself apart internally.

---

# 4. The Sundering

At the height of the siege, Aldren began the preservation ritual.

His goal was to lock Caer Veyr into the final moment before defeat.

The ritual was interrupted.

The Crownheart did not preserve a single moment.

Instead, it split the castle's remembered state.

Two dominant spatial memories survived.

## The Past

Caer Veyr close to the kingdom's final days.

It is:

- structurally intact;
- lit;
- furnished;
- occupied by guards and temporal inhabitants;
- filled with functioning mechanisms;
- militarized;
- beautiful but uneasy.

This is not a peaceful golden age.

It is the castle shortly before catastrophe.

## The Present

Caer Veyr generations later.

It is:

- fractured;
- partially collapsed;
- overgrown;
- weathered;
- abandoned by ordinary society;
- reshaped by erosion and destruction;
- filled with temporal remnants produced by the Crownheart.

Both states occupy the same castle.

Neither is fully real in the ordinary sense.

They are stable spatial memories enforced by the Crownheart.

This event became known as **the Sundering**.

---

# 5. The Protagonist

The protagonist descends from House Vaelor but was never part of the recognized succession line.

Their ancestor was an illegitimate royal child who escaped the formal blood-binding rites.

This matters.

The protagonist still carries enough Vaelor blood for the Crownheart to recognize them.

But their lineage was never fully bound into the royal ritual structure.

They are connected without being trapped.

That makes them the first person capable of forcing the Crownheart to change which memory of the castle dominates.

They are the **Uncrowned**.

The protagonist initially returns to Caer Veyr believing the castle is their stolen inheritance.

Their motivation is simple:

> Enter the ancestral stronghold. Survive it. Discover what remains. Reclaim what was denied to the bloodline.

The game gradually destroys that assumption.

---

# 6. What Time Shifting Actually Is

The protagonist does not literally travel backward and forward through universal history.

They manipulate the Crownheart.

When the player shifts time, they force the castle to answer a question:

> Which remembered state is dominant here?

That is why:

- the player's physical position persists;
- architecture changes around them;
- destroyed structures can reconstruct;
- intact structures can collapse;
- furnishings can return or vanish;
- light and atmosphere can transform;
- enemies can differ between states.

The 2 to 3 second hold represents the protagonist overpowering the Crownheart's current state.

---

# 7. Why Combat Recharges Time Power

The Sundering left thousands of human imprints embedded in the castle.

These are called **Echoes**.

An Echo is a preserved pattern of a person caught in the Crownheart's memory.

Some behave almost like the person they once were.

Others have degraded into repetitive soldiers, guards, intruders, or violent remnants.

Combat destabilizes these imprints.

When an Echo is defeated, a portion of its stored temporal resonance is released.

The protagonist's blood can absorb that resonance.

This is the lore basis for the gameplay loop:

> Fight enemies -> release resonance -> recharge temporal power -> shift the castle -> progress.

The player is therefore not collecting arbitrary mana.

They are reclaiming energy trapped inside the Sundering.

---

# 8. Enemy Logic

The game can support many enemies without needing a living modern army occupying the ruin.

## Past enemies

Past-state opponents may include:

- royal guards who identify the protagonist as an unauthorized blood claimant;
- soldiers obeying Aldren's emergency orders;
- rebels or mutineers;
- warped guards already influenced by the Crownheart;
- elite royal wardens protecting sealed sections.

They exist as preserved memory, not as ordinary people who can permanently rewrite history.

## Present enemies

Present opponents are more degraded manifestations:

- Echo Guards;
- Hollow Wardens;
- armored remnants repeatedly reenacting defensive behavior;
- unstable temporal soldiers;
- stronger Crownheart-bound entities near the lower levels.

This allows the visual language to become increasingly unnatural as the player approaches the Crownheart.

---

# 9. What the Player Gradually Learns

At first, environmental evidence suggests:

> An invading army destroyed Veyr.

Then contradictions appear.

Examples of narrative clues:

- doors barricaded from inside;
- royal guards positioned against other royal guards;
- sealed servant routes;
- ritual markings hidden beneath ceremonial architecture;
- records ordering civilians into restricted wings;
- damaged royal symbols;
- bodies or echoes where no battle with outsiders should have occurred;
- evidence that people were trying to escape the king.

The player eventually realizes:

> The siege was real, but the king caused the catastrophe.

---

# 10. The Final King

King Aldren did not survive in an ordinary sense.

The Crownheart preserved him more strongly than anyone else because:

- he was the reigning bound monarch;
- he was physically near the Crownheart;
- he was directing the ritual when the Sundering occurred.

His imprint became fused with the mechanism.

Over generations, that imprint lost the distinction between:

- the king;
- the castle;
- the royal bloodline;
- the idea of preserving Veyr forever.

The final manifestation is called **the Last Crown**.

It believes the kingdom can still be saved.

To it, the ruined Present is a temporary error.

The protagonist is valuable because their unbound royal blood may be able to finish the original ritual.

The final antagonist does not simply want to kill the player.

It wants to use them.

---

# 11. Three-Floor Narrative Arc

This is narrative pacing only. It does not define the map layout.

Claude must design the actual rooms and corridors independently.

## Floor 1: Inheritance

Narrative purpose:

- establish the castle;
- establish the blood connection;
- teach Past/Present;
- show that the Past is not safe;
- introduce Echo enemies;
- give the player reasons to believe the family history may be false.

The emotional question:

> What happened here?

## Floor 2: Complicity

Narrative purpose:

- expose evidence of the preservation ritual;
- show conflict inside House Vaelor;
- reveal that civilians and soldiers were deliberately trapped;
- make the player question the idea of reclaiming the throne.

The emotional question:

> What did my family do?

## Floor 3: The Crownheart

Floor 3 is intentionally shorter.

Narrative purpose:

- collapse the distinction between Past and Present;
- reveal Aldren's surviving imprint;
- enter the Crownheart's immediate influence;
- confront the Last Crown.

The emotional question:

> Should this inheritance survive at all?

---

# 12. Final Confrontation

The boss is **the Last Crown**, the Crownheart's preserved manifestation of King Aldren.

The encounter should make the temporal mechanic relevant rather than becoming a normal sword fight with a large health bar.

Possible design principles for Claude to develop later:

- attack patterns differ by temporal state;
- arena geometry changes;
- cover or traversal opportunities differ;
- the boss may forcibly destabilize the current state;
- player combat is still central;
- switching state should create tactical opportunity but require earned charge.

Do not fully design the boss until Floors 1 and 2 have proven the core systems.

---

# 13. Tone

The game is dark medieval fantasy, but not pure horror.

Desired tone:

- regal;
- tragic;
- mysterious;
- physical;
- grounded;
- grand;
- haunted by history.

Avoid:

- comedy-first fantasy;
- excessive magical particle clutter;
- generic glowing-rune-everywhere fantasy;
- exposition dumps;
- constant voiceover.

Tell the story through architecture, state differences, enemy placement, objects, and short discoveries.

---

# 14. Environmental Storytelling Rule

Whenever possible, story information should be visible through comparison.

Example principle:

Past:
- a door is guarded and sealed.

Present:
- the same door is broken outward.

The player should naturally ask why.

The strongest lore moments should come from seeing the same place in two states and realizing that the official version of history cannot explain both.
